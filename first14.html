Field guide · Landing without a room

Fourteen days to a signed contract

You land in three weeks with nowhere to live. That is normal, it is survivable, and the whole thing turns on one piece of paper you have to ask for on the day you sign. Here is the plan, with the actual places to book.

Do not book a hostel for 30 days. You will spend the money and still have no Anmeldung at the end of it.

01

The fourteen-day plan

Four phases. The dates matter because each one only works if the one before it is done.

Before you fly

Book 10–14 nights. Not 30.

Long bookings feel safe and cost you a month's rent for nothing. Book short, extend if you need to. Almost nobody needs the full month.

Day 1–3

Apply to twenty ads

Twenty, not five. Most will never reply and that is completely normal — it is a volume game, not a quality one.

Day 4–10

Viewings

A WG viewing is not a house tour. It is an interview, and they are picking you. Prepare for it like one.

Day 10–14

Sign — and ask for the paper

On the same day you sign, ask for the Wohnungsgeberbestätigung. Do not leave without it.

Why fourteen and not thirty

A 30-night hostel booking at typical rates runs into the high hundreds of euros, and it does not move you one step closer to an Anmeldung. That same money is roughly a month's rent, or a large chunk of a deposit. Book short deliberately — the pressure is what makes you send twenty applications instead of five.

02

Where to stay for the first two weeks

Real chains and platforms, with what they cost. Book a dorm bed, not a private room — you are sleeping there, not living there.

Prices checked August 2026

Dorm beds across Germany average around €20 a night, with a wide spread by city and date. September is peak arrival season, so book earlier than you think and expect the top of each range.

The chains worth knowing

DJH — Deutsches Jugendherbergswerk jugendherberge.de ~400 locations

Germany's official youth hostel network, and the one nobody outside Germany has heard of. Present in small university towns where commercial hostels don't exist, which matters if you're going somewhere like Aachen, Göttingen or Marburg rather than Berlin. You need a cheap DJH membership to book — factor that in, it pays for itself in a few nights.

A&O Hostels aohostels.com Near main stations

Large chain, usually a short walk from the Hauptbahnhof, which is exactly what you want when you're carrying two suitcases and don't know the city. Reliably cheap, reliably basic. Locations in most major German cities.

MEININGER meininger-hotels.com Hostel–hotel hybrid

A step up in comfort and price. Dorm beds alongside private rooms, usually with a guest kitchen — which genuinely matters if you're cooking for two weeks rather than eating out. Good if you're arriving with family who need a private room.

Wombat's · Generator Berlin, Munich, Hamburg Social hostels

Backpacker-style, with bars and common rooms. Worth considering for a different reason than price: you will meet people, and in your first week those people are how you hear about rooms before they're advertised. Big-city only.

Where to book

  • Hostelworld and Booking.com — compare both, prices differ for the same bed.
  • Book direct with the chain once you've found the place. It's often cheaper and cancellation terms are usually better.
  • Filter for free cancellation. Non-negotiable. If a room comes through on day 6 you want to walk away from nights 7 to 14 without paying for them.
The limit of a hostel

You cannot do your Anmeldung from a normal hostel. No registration means no bank account, no phone contract and no residence permit. A hostel is a place to sleep while you hunt — it is never the destination.

03

Zwischenmiete — the better option if you can get one

A sublet for a few weeks or months. Cheaper than a hostel per night, you get a real room, and some of them will register you.

A Zwischenmiete is a temporary sublet — someone's away for a semester, or between tenants. It is the single best landing pad if you can find one, because you get a proper room at proper rent instead of a bunk at tourist rates.

Where to look

  • 01WG-Gesucht.deThe main German flat-share site, free to use. Filter on "befristet" — limited-term — and set the duration from two weeks to three months. This is where most Zwischenmiete listings live.
  • 02HousingAnywhereBuilt for international students, mid-term stays, listings are verified and you can book from abroad. Costs more than WG-Gesucht but the scam risk is far lower.
  • 03WunderflatsFurnished mid-term rentals with verified landlords. Pricier again, but many listings explicitly allow registration — check for "Anmeldung möglich" in the listing.
  • 04Studenten-WG.deSmaller than WG-Gesucht but less crowded, so your application competes against fewer people.
  • 05Your university's WohnungsbörseMost German universities run a housing noticeboard for their own students. Almost nobody arriving from abroad knows it exists. Search your university's name plus "Wohnungsbörse".
  • 06City Facebook groupsSearch "WG Zimmer" plus your city. Rooms appear here before they hit the big sites — but so do the scams, so never send money to anyone you found this way without a live video call.
The question that decides everything

Ask every temporary landlord one thing before you book: "Kann ich mich hier anmelden?" — can I register here? If the answer is yes, that sublet is worth paying more for, because it unlocks your bank account, your SIM and your residence permit. If it's no, it's just an expensive bed.

And apply to the Studentenwerk anyway

Even if the waiting list looks hopeless. Rooms open up constantly when people decline their offers, and you cannot be offered one if you never applied. It costs you ten minutes.

04

Day 1–3: twenty applications

Not five. Twenty. The reply rate is low for everyone, including Germans.

Most of your messages will never be answered. That is not about you — a single popular WG ad in a university city can receive well over a hundred replies within a day. Your job is volume plus a message that isn't obviously copy-pasted.

What to put in the message

  • Your name, age, what you study and where. First line, no preamble.
  • Your move-in date and how long you want to stay. Long-term is more attractive than short.
  • How the rent is paid — blocked account, scholarship, parents. This is the thing they actually worry about.
  • One or two real sentences about you. Whether you cook, whether you're loud, what you do at weekends. They're choosing a flatmate, not a tenant.
  • A photo of yourself. German WG culture expects it. A message with no photo reads as a bot.
  • Reference the ad. One line proving you read it beats three paragraphs proving you didn't.
Write it in German if you can

Even shaky German, with a line saying you're still learning. A German-language message goes to the top of the pile in a shared flat where everyone speaks German. If your German isn't there yet, write in English and say so plainly rather than running a whole message through a translator badly.

05

Day 4–10: the viewing is an interview

You are not inspecting the flat. They are inspecting you, usually alongside five other candidates the same evening.

A WG-Casting is exactly what the name says. You will often be one of several people invited on the same night, sometimes all at once. The room is not the question — you are.

What decides it

  • Turn up on time. In Germany this is not a small thing. Five minutes late can end it before you speak.
  • Ask about them, not just the room. How do they split cleaning, do they eat together, what's the noise like. It signals you're thinking about living with people.
  • Be honest about your German. Overstating it always surfaces later and it costs you trust immediately.
  • Have your documents ready — passport, admission letter, blocked account confirmation. Being able to produce them on the spot puts you ahead of most applicants.
  • Say you want to stay long-term. Turnover is the thing they hate most.
  • Follow up the next day. One short message. Most people don't, and it's remembered.
Never

Never pay a viewing fee. Charging you to see a flat is illegal in Germany. Anyone asking is either running a scam or breaking the law — either way, walk.

06

Day 10–14: sign, and ask for the paper

This is the part of the reel people will rewatch. Everything downstream depends on it.

The document is the Wohnungsgeberbestätigung — landlord's confirmation of residence. It is a short form your landlord signs confirming you actually moved in. Without it, the Bürgeramt will not register you, full stop.

You sign

Ask the same day

Do not leave the signing without it, or at minimum without a firm date. Chasing a landlord for it later is how people lose a month.

Then

Anmeldung

Book your Bürgeramt appointment the moment you have a move-in date. Slots run weeks ahead in most cities.

Then

Bank + SIM contract

Both need your registration certificate. Neither happens before it.

Then

Residence permit

The Ausländerbehörde needs your Anmeldung too. This is the one with a real deadline attached.

The whole chain hangs on one signature

No Wohnungsgeberbestätigung → no Anmeldung → no bank account, no phone contract, no residence permit. One form, signed by one person, on the day you sign your contract. Ask for it in the room.

If your landlord hesitates or doesn't know what you mean, the form is standard and downloadable from most city websites — bring a printed copy to the signing and hand it to them.

07

Traps

Booking 30 nights up front

Hundreds of euros for a bed that gets you no closer to registration. Book 10–14 with free cancellation.

Applying to five ads

Popular listings get over a hundred replies. Five applications is not a search, it's a wish.

Booking without free cancellation

If a room comes through on day six you want to walk away from the rest without paying.

Signing a lease from abroad

Landlords can't verify someone who isn't in the country, and remote applicants lose to in-person ones every time.

Paying before you sign

Kaution is capped at three months' cold rent and is paid after signature. Money before a contract is how deposits disappear.

Leaving without the Wohnungsgeberbestätigung

Chasing it afterwards costs weeks. Ask in the room, on the day.

Assuming a hostel address works

You cannot register from a normal hostel. Ask every temporary landlord whether registration is possible.

Arriving during a city event

Oktoberfest in Munich, trade fairs in Cologne. Hostel prices multiply and beds vanish. Check the city calendar before booking.

08

Questions

What if I don't find anything in 14 days?

Extend the hostel in short blocks and keep applying. Fourteen days is a realistic target, not a guarantee — competition varies enormously between Leipzig and Munich. What matters is that you're applying at volume the whole time, not that you hit the date.

Can I register at a hostel?

Not at a normal one. Some longer-stay providers and serviced apartments will register you — that's exactly why you ask "Kann ich mich hier anmelden?" before booking anything longer term.

Is a Zwischenmiete better than a hostel?

Almost always. Cheaper per night, a real room, and some allow registration. The catch is they're harder to get and carry more scam risk, so verify the listing and insist on a live video call before paying anything.

How much deposit will I need?

Kaution is capped by law at three months' cold rent. It is paid after you sign, by bank transfer. Anyone asking for cash before a signature is a warning sign.

Should I book a hostel or an Airbnb?

Hostel dorms are cheaper for one person. Airbnb makes sense if you're arriving with family, or for a longer stay where the monthly discount beats nightly hostel rates — but check whether registration is possible before you commit.

Do I really need twenty applications?

Yes. Most go unanswered, and that's true for German students too. Treat the first fifteen as practice for the five that reply.

What documents should I carry to a viewing?

Passport, admission letter, and your blocked account or funding confirmation. Being able to produce them immediately puts you ahead of most people in the room.
