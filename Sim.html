Getting a phone number in Germany

Every German SIM has to be registered to your name with an ID check you do online. Which needs data. Which you don't have yet. This page is the way around that, plus what every provider actually charges.

You need internet to get internet. Sort that out before you fly, not at the airport.

01

The order this happens in

Four stages. Doing them out of order is what strands people for a week with no number.

Before you fly

Buy a travel eSIM

Bought and installed in India, activates the moment you land. No German paperwork, no address, no ID check.

Day 1–2

Buy a prepaid starter pack

€5–10 at any supermarket or kiosk. This gets you a German number — the thing appointments, banks and delivery drivers actually need.

Day 1–5

Verify your identity

VideoIdent from your room or PostIdent at a Deutsche Post branch. Until this is done the SIM will not work.

Month 2–3

Switch, if it's worth it

Once you have your Anmeldung and a German bank account, a contract or SIM-only plan may be cheaper. Only then.

02

What you need to hand

For a prepaid SIM. Nothing here requires your Anmeldung.

  • 01Your passportThe physical document, not a photo. Video verification asks you to tilt and turn it under a light.
  • 02A German street addressYour hostel, Airbnb, dorm or a friend's place is fine. This is where the operator files your registration — it is not proof of residence and does not need to match your Anmeldung.
  • 03An unlocked phonePhones bought on Indian carrier financing are often network-locked. Check before you fly, not after.
  • 04An internet connection for the ID checkYour travel eSIM, or hostel wifi. This is the step that traps people.
  • 05Cash or a working card€10–20 for the starter pack. Some kiosks won't take foreign cards.
Good news

A prepaid SIM needs identity verification, not residency. You do not need a German bank account, a registered address, or a residence permit to get a working German number in your first week.

03

The eSIM you buy before you fly

This is a bridge, not a destination. It buys you data for the first few days so you can do everything else.

A travel eSIM is bought online, installed on your phone as a QR code while you are still in India, and switches on when you land. There is no ID check and no address, because you are buying a tourist data plan, not a German line.

What to look for

  • Your phone must support eSIM. Most iPhones from XS onward and most recent Android flagships do. Many mid-range Android phones sold in India do not. Check in Settings before you buy anything.
  • Buy 7–15 days, not 30. You only need it until your German SIM is verified. Paying for a month is money you don't get back.
  • Data-only is usually enough. Calls go over WhatsApp. What you cannot do on a data-only eSIM is receive an SMS one-time password — which matters if an Indian bank texts you codes.
  • Install and test it in India. Scan the QR while you still have a working connection. Installing an eSIM with no internet is its own trap.
Check the number

eSIM providers change prices constantly and run different rates for different countries. Compare at least two on the day you buy rather than trusting any list, including this one. Commonly used options for Europe include Airalo, Saily, Simbye and JamboSim — no endorsement, and the cheapest one changes month to month.

The alternative: international roaming from India

Airtel and Jio both sell international roaming packs. They are usually more expensive per GB than a travel eSIM, but they keep your Indian number live — which is worth paying for if your Indian bank, your blocked account provider or your university still sends OTPs to it. Many people run both: Indian number on roaming for OTPs, eSIM for data.

Germany has three physical networks — Telekom, Vodafone and O2 — and every other brand rents capacity from one of them. The brand you buy determines the price. The network determines whether you have signal on the train.

05

How activation actually works

Since 2017, German law requires every SIM to be registered against a verified identity document. There is no way around this and no unregistered SIM sold anywhere.

  1. Buy the starter packA card in a cardboard sleeve, sold at supermarkets, petrol stations, kiosks and phone shops. It contains a SIM and an activation code. Buying it is not activating it.
  2. Register onlineGo to the provider's activation page, enter the code from the pack, and fill in your name, date of birth and a German address. Hostel or temporary address is fine here.
  3. Verify your identityChoose one of the two methods below. This is where you need an internet connection.
  4. Wait for the SMSUsually minutes, sometimes a few hours. Your number arrives by text or in the app.
  5. Top up and buy a packageCredit alone does nothing useful. You have to actively select a data package — otherwise you're billed per megabyte at brutal rates.

VideoIdent / WebID

A live video call with an agent. You show your passport, tilt it under the light, and answer a couple of questions. Takes about ten minutes.

Hours: roughly 8am–10pm.
You need: a stable connection and decent light.
Do it from: your room, on your travel eSIM or hostel wifi.

PostIdent

You generate a coupon, take it and your passport to a Deutsche Post branch, and a clerk verifies you in person.

Hours: post office opening hours only.
You need: the printed or on-screen coupon.
Use this if: video verification keeps failing, which happens often with worn passports or bad lighting.

The one that catches people

VideoIdent needs internet. If you land with a dead Indian SIM and no eSIM, you cannot verify, which means you cannot activate, which means you have no German number. Hostel wifi works — but if you land at night, arrive somewhere without wifi, or your accommodation falls through, you are stuck until morning with no way to call anyone.

That is the entire reason to buy the travel eSIM in India.

Buying at the airport instead

You can buy a SIM at most German airports, and the shop staff will verify you in person on the spot — which solves the internet problem. The trade-off is price: airport packs are usually tourist-priced and worse value than the same brand from a supermarket. It's a reasonable fallback, not a plan.

07

Paying for it in the first week

The connectivity gap has a money twin, and they collide on the same days.

Your blocked account is not the same thing as a usable bank account. Depending on the provider and how fast your documents clear, the card can take days to weeks to arrive and activate after you land. Meanwhile you need to pay for a SIM, groceries, transport and a deposit.

What to arrange before you fly

  • A card that works in Germany. A forex/travel card loaded in euros, or an international debit card you have specifically confirmed works abroad. Tell your Indian bank you're travelling so they don't block it.
  • Some cash. Germany is far more cash-dependent than India. Bakeries, kiosks, some doctors and plenty of small restaurants still refuse cards. Carry euros within your legal declaration limits.
  • Know your card's fees. Loading fee, per-transaction fee, ATM fee, and the exchange rate applied. These vary enormously and are the entire difference between forex products.
  • A backup. One card, one failure, no money. Carry a second payment method stored separately.
Not advice

This page deliberately doesn't recommend a specific forex card or bank. Fees, rates and eligibility change constantly and depend on your bank, so compare on the day rather than trusting any list. Check what you'll actually be charged before you load money onto anything.

Why the German number matters more than the data

In week one, the number is the point. Bürgeramt appointment confirmations, bank verification codes, package delivery notices, landlords replying to your messages, university systems — a surprising number of them will only take a German mobile number. Data you can borrow from wifi. A number you cannot.

08

Traps

The specific ways this goes wrong.

Credit is not a package

Topping up €15 does not give you data. You have to select a package in the app. Otherwise you burn the credit at per-megabyte rates in a day.

28 days, not a month

Packages renew every 28 days, so you pay 13 times a year. Budget accordingly, and know your renewal date.

Auto-renewal needs credit

If your balance is too low on renewal day, the package silently doesn't renew and you're on expensive pay-as-you-go without noticing.

Locked phones

A handset bought on an Indian carrier plan may be network-locked. Unlock it in India — doing it from Germany is far harder.

Failed video verification

Worn passports, bad lighting and unstable connections all fail VideoIdent. Switch to PostIdent rather than retrying five times.

Coverage isn't uniform

O2 is noticeably weaker on regional rail and in rural areas. If you're in a small university town, check the coverage map for your postcode before choosing a brand.

Ordering a SIM online on day one

Free online SIMs need a delivery address and several days of post. Useless if you land tomorrow. Buy physical in a shop first.

Signing a 24-month contract early

Before you know which city you'll settle in, what your data use really is, or whether you'll stay after graduation. Prepaid first.

09

Questions

Can I get a German SIM without an Anmeldung?

Yes. Prepaid SIMs need identity verification, not proof of residence. You enter an address during registration, but a hostel, Airbnb, dorm or friend's address is accepted. Contracts are the ones that require your registration certificate.

Can I buy a German SIM from India before I fly?

Not usefully. You can order some SIMs to a German address, but you can't complete identity verification from outside the country and you have nowhere to receive the post. Buy a travel eSIM instead — that's exactly the problem it solves.

Do I need to keep my Indian number?

Usually yes, at least for the first few months. Indian banks, your blocked account provider, DigiLocker and various government services send one-time passwords by SMS to it. Keep it on a minimal roaming or validity plan rather than letting it lapse.

Which network has the best coverage?

Telekom, consistently — especially on regional trains and outside cities. Vodafone is close in urban areas. O2 is the cheapest to buy into and the weakest in rural coverage. If you're going to a small town, that difference is worth the extra euro or two a month.

How much data do I actually need?

Most students land somewhere between 10 and 25 GB a month, and university, home and most cafés have wifi. Start at the 25 GB tier that costs about €9 and adjust after your first month, when you can see real usage in the app.

Is 5G worth paying more for?

No. The discounter brands include 5G with a speed cap around 100 Mbit/s, which is more than enough for anything you'll do on a phone. Don't pay a premium for uncapped 5G.

What if my verification keeps failing?

Switch method. If VideoIdent fails twice, generate a PostIdent coupon and go to a Deutsche Post branch with your passport. In-person verification almost never fails.

Can I keep my German number if I switch providers?

Yes — number portability is a legal right in Germany, including from prepaid. You request it from your old provider when you leave. Some charge a fee, some are required to pay you for it; check the current rules when you switch.
