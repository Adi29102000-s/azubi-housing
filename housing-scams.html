Before you send the deposit

The housing fraud checklist for students moving to Germany.

Every year students lose four-figure sums on flats that do not exist.

The scam is always the same shape: a great room, a landlord who cannot meet you, and a payment method with no way back. Nine minutes of checking is what stands between you and a transfer you cannot reverse.

Six things a scammer says

If you hear any one of these, stop and verify before you reply.

  • “I'm currently abroad. I'll courier you the keys.” Keys are handed over in person, at the flat, on move-in day. No real landlord in Germany posts them.
  • “Send the deposit and the flat is yours.” Money moves after you have seen the flat and signed the Mietvertrag. Never before both.
  • “My camera is broken, I can't do a video call.” A refused live video call is the single most reliable sign of a fake listing. Real landlords will show you the rooms.
  • “Pay by PayPal Friends & Family / crypto / Western Union.” All three are chosen because they have no buyer protection and no trace. The money is gone the moment it leaves.
  • “Three others want it. Decide tonight.” Manufactured urgency exists to stop you checking. The German market is tight, but real landlords still wait for a viewing.
  • “Just send your passport and visa scans first.” Some listings are not after your deposit at all. They are collecting documents to open accounts in your name.

How the money moves

  • ✓SEPA bank transfer only, to a German IBAN that starts with DE, held in the landlord's or company's name.
  • ✓Pay on the day you sign and collect keys — not before.
  • ✓Keep the transfer receipt and the signed contract together.
  • ×Never PayPal Friends & Family, Western Union, MoneyGram, crypto, or gift cards.
  • ×Never a foreign IBAN for a German flat.
  • ×Never a fee to view a flat, apply for it, or “reserve” it. Those fees do not exist here.

Two rules worth knowing

The deposit is capped at three months' cold rent
Kaution may not exceed three times the Kaltmiete (§551 BGB), and you are entitled to pay it in three monthly instalments. Anyone demanding five months up front is either a scammer or breaking the law.
You do not pay the agent's fee unless you hired the agent
Under the Bestellerprinzip, whoever engaged the Makler pays them. If the landlord found the agent, the commission is not yours.

If you have already sent the money

Speed decides whether any of it comes back. Do these in order, today.

  1. Call your bank now

    Ask for a SEPA recall on the transfer. A recall only has a chance in the first hours or days, so this comes before everything else.

  2. File a police report

    Every German state has an online Onlinewache where you can report fraud without going in. You need the report number for the bank and for any insurance claim.

  3. Report the listing to the platform

    It stops the same advert reaching the next student, and platforms keep records that help the investigation.

  4. Save every piece of evidence

    Screenshots of the listing and the full chat, the email headers, the transfer confirmation, the IBAN and the name on it. Do this before anything gets deleted.

  5. Tell your university's international office

    They handle this every semester, often know the specific scam, and can usually point you to emergency accommodation.
