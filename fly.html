Medicine & customs

Flying to Germany with medicine in your bag

The rules are stricter than India's and far less scary than the internet says. Here is what German customs law actually allows.

3 months
Per medicine, for your own use

That is the legal limit. Within it you need no import permit, and it makes no difference whether the medicine is prescription-only or over the counter, or whether it is approved in Germany at all.

Clearing up a myth first You do not need a stamped doctor's prescription to carry ordinary medicine into Germany. Paracetamol, cold strips, antacids, your regular tablets — a personal supply is legal. The German Health Ministry and Zoll both state this plainly. The rules that do bite are narrower, and they are further down this page.
1

What you can bring without any paperwork

  • Up to a three-month supply of each medicine, counted using the dosage on the leaflet.
  • Prescription-only or over-the-counter — the law makes no distinction for travel.
  • Medicines not approved in Germany, including Indian brands with no German equivalent.
  • Ayurvedic and homeopathic preparations, within the same personal-use limit.
The two conditions It must be for you, and it must be a supply, not a stock. Carrying medicine for your cousin, your roommate or "in case someone needs it" is outside the exception, and so is a year's worth of anything.
2

What actually causes problems

⚠️ Controlled substances This is the real one. Anything classed as a narcotic under German law — codeine cough syrups, tramadol, alprazolam and similar sedatives, ADHD medication, strong opioid painkillers — has its own rules and needs a signed doctor's certificate in English. Several of these are sold over the counter in India and are strictly controlled here. Certificate required
📦 More than three months, or for someone else Stockpiling is explicitly excluded, as is bringing medicine on behalf of another person. This is the most common way a normal traveller falls outside the rule without meaning to. Not covered
❌ Counterfeit or unlabelled medicine There is no personal-use exception for fakes. Loose tablets in a plastic pouch with no box and no leaflet are hard to identify and easy to seize. Keep everything in its original packaging. Always prohibited
🌿 Ayurvedic products — a genuine caveat Legal as personal supply, but some traditional preparations have been found to contain heavy metals or undeclared pharmaceutical ingredients. Those can be seized as unsafe regardless of quantity. Sealed, branded, labelled products from a reputable manufacturer are far safer than loose powders in a home-packed jar. Bring sealed & labelled
💪 Anabolic steroids and doping agents Prohibited under separate anti-doping law, with no travel exception. Do not bring gym supplements of uncertain composition. Always prohibited
The part people confuse A prescription is always required for one specific thing: ordering medicine from outside the EU through a German pharmacy after you arrive. That is mail order, not luggage. Guides that mix the two up are the reason people think they need a stamped prescription at the airport.
3

How to pack it

  • Original boxes with the leaflet inside. The leaflet proves the dosage, which is how the three-month limit is calculated.
  • Hand luggage, not checked bags, for anything you actually need. Hold luggage gets lost and pharmacies here will not replace a foreign prescription.
  • A doctor's letter in English for regular medication. Not legally required for ordinary medicines, but it ends any conversation in thirty seconds. Template below.
  • Declare it if asked, and answer plainly. Nothing you are carrying legally becomes a problem by mentioning it.
  • Photograph everything before you fly — boxes, leaflets, the letter. Useful if a bag goes missing.
Worth knowing Most common medicines are cheap and easy to get in Germany. Ibuprofen, paracetamol and cold remedies are all available in any Apotheke without a prescription. You do not need to bring a year of supplies — bring what you need for the transition and buy the rest here.
4

Doctor's letter template

Have your doctor put this on their letterhead, sign it, and stamp it. Carry the original with your medicines. For controlled substances this is not optional.

Medical certificate for travel English · signed and stamped by your doctor 
MEDICAL CERTIFICATE FOR TRAVEL

To whom it may concern,

I confirm that I am the treating physician of:

Name of patient:      [FULL NAME AS IN PASSPORT]
Date of birth:        [DD/MM/YYYY]
Passport number:      [PASSPORT NUMBER]

The patient is under my care for the following condition:
[DIAGNOSIS IN PLAIN ENGLISH]

The patient requires the following medication, which is
necessary for continuous personal treatment during travel
and residence abroad:

1. [GENERIC / ACTIVE INGREDIENT NAME]
   Brand name:  [BRAND]
   Strength:    [e.g. 500 mg]
   Dosage:      [e.g. one tablet twice daily]
   Quantity carried: [e.g. 180 tablets — 90 days supply]

2. [REPEAT FOR EACH MEDICINE]

The quantities carried correspond to the patient's personal
requirement for a maximum of three months at the prescribed
dosage. The medication is for the patient's own use only.

Physician:      Dr. [FULL NAME]
Registration:   [MEDICAL COUNCIL REGISTRATION NUMBER]
Clinic:         [CLINIC NAME AND FULL ADDRESS]
Telephone:      [WITH COUNTRY CODE]
Email:          [EMAIL]

Signature: ______________________     Date: [DD/MM/YYYY]

[OFFICIAL STAMP OF THE CLINIC]
Use the generic name Write the active ingredient, not just the Indian brand name. A German officer or pharmacist will not recognise most Indian brands, but every one of them knows paracetamol, cetirizine or pantoprazole. This single detail prevents most of the confusion at a customs desk.

Bring a supply, not a stockpile

Three months of what you actually take, in the original boxes, with a signed English letter for anything regular. That is the whole rule. Everything beyond that is either unnecessary or the thing that gets you stopped.

Based on §73 of the German Medicines Act (Arzneimittelgesetz) as summarised by the German Federal Ministry of Health and German customs (Zoll), September 2026. Controlled substances are governed separately under narcotics law. This is general information, not medical or legal advice — for anything you depend on, confirm with the German embassy in your country before you fly.
