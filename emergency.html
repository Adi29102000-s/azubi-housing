Numbers, doctors and the German words you need when something goes wrong.

Call right now

Life threateningAmbulance & Fire

Serious injury, chest pain, unconsciousness, heavy bleeding, an accident, a fire. Works anywhere in the EU. Free from any phone.

112 Ambulance · Fire brigade · Rescue services
PolicePolizei

Crime in progress, theft, assault, harassment, someone threatening you, a break-in. If you are unsure whether it is police or ambulance, call 112 — they will transfer you.

110 Free · 24 hours · Every network
Not an emergencyOn-call doctor

You are ill but it is not life threatening, and it is night, a weekend or a public holiday when normal practices are closed. Fever, infection, bad pain, a sick note. They tell you where to go or send a doctor.

116 117 Free · Dial with no area code · 24 hours
Read this before you need it In Germany you cannot call 112 or 110 without a SIM card in the phone. This was blocked nationwide in 2009 because of misuse — so the advice you have read elsewhere does not apply here. The SIM does not need credit and the phone does not need to be unlocked, and it will borrow any network that has signal. But an empty phone will not dial. Get a SIM in week one.

Other numbers worth saving

MoneyBlock a lost bank card

Wallet stolen or card gone. One number blocks German debit and credit cards, and it works day and night. Report the theft to the police as well.

116 116
PharmacyNight & weekend pharmacy

German pharmacies close in the evening, but one in each area stays open on rota. This line tells you which one is open near you right now.

0800 0022 833 Free from a German landline or mobile
SupportViolence against women helpline

Free, anonymous, 24 hours, and available in around 18 languages with an interpreter — including Hindi, Urdu, Arabic and English. For domestic violence, sexual violence, stalking or harassment.

116 016
SupportSomeone to talk to

Free, anonymous and open all night, for anyone who is struggling — homesickness, stress, or feeling like it is all too much. Mostly German-speaking; your university also has a free psychological counselling service (Psychologische Beratung) that usually offers English.

0800 111 0 111
Poisoning Germany has no single national poison number — each region runs its own Giftnotruf. Search “Giftnotruf” plus your state now and save it. If someone has swallowed something dangerous and is unwell, call 112 and do not wait to look it up.

What to say when they answer

Operators are trained to lead the conversation. Many speak English — if you are stuck, say “Do you speak English?” and wait. Do not hang up until they tell you to.

  1. Where. Street, number, city, floor. This matters most — say it first, before anything else.
  2. What happened. One plain sentence. “A man collapsed.” “There is a fire.”
  3. How many people are hurt.
  4. What kind of injury or symptom, as best you can describe it.
  5. Wait. Stay on the line and answer their questions. They will end the call.

Take your insurance card Bring your Versichertenkarte to every appointment and to hospital. Without it you may be asked to pay up front and claim it back later. If it is a real emergency, go anyway — nobody is turned away from an emergency room for missing a card.
