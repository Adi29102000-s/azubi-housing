Germany · Arrival · Short-term stay

Land first.
Find a room after.

Trying to sign a lease from India almost never works. Book two to six weeks somewhere cheap, then search on the ground — that's how most students actually do it.

Read this before you book anything

You cannot register your address (Anmeldung) from a normal hostel. Without Anmeldung you can't get a tax ID, a regular bank account, or finalise your residence permit. So a hostel is a landing pad, not a home — and German law expects you to register within 14 days of moving into a real address.

Your options, cheapest first

Prices are typical ranges, not quotes. They swing hard by city and by season — October and April are the worst weeks of the year to arrive.

  • Youth hostel (Jugendherberge)
    €20–35 / night

    The cheapest safe option. Dorm beds, shared bathrooms, usually clean and well run. Membership of the Youth Hostel Association gets you a lower rate — check whether your home country's YHA membership works before you pay for a German one.

    German Youth Hostels ↗ Hostelworld ↗
  • Independent hostels
    €25–45 / night

    More of them, more central, more variable. Filter for a kitchen — cooking for two weeks instead of eating out will save you more than the room price difference.

    Hostelworld ↗ Booking.com ↗
  • Zwischenmiete (sublet)
    varies

    A student going abroad sublets their room for a few months. Often the best value of anything on this page, and sometimes the sublet turns into the permanent room. Listed on WG-Gesucht and in city Facebook groups.

    WG-Gesucht ↗
    Ask directly whether you can register your address. Some sublets allow it, most don't.
  • Furnished platforms
    €600–1,200 / month

    Expensive, but you can book from India with a real contract and no viewing. Worth it if you need certainty more than you need cheap.

    HousingAnywhere ↗ Wunderflats ↗
    Filter for "Anmeldung possible" or "Wohnungsgeberbestätigung". Without it, the whole point is lost.
  • Your university's International Office
    often free to ask

    The option nobody uses. Many universities keep emergency or orientation-week accommodation for arriving international students, and student services sometimes offer beds for the first few nights. Email them before you fly — this costs you one message.

What to actually do

If you're flying in the next few weeks with nothing booked, work through this in order.

  1. Book two to six weeks, not two nights
    Berlin students routinely search six to eight weeks. Two nights of booking puts you back in a panic on day three.
  2. Email your International Office today
    Ask about orientation-week accommodation and any emergency housing list. Do it before you book anything else.
  3. Apply to your Studentenwerk anyway
    Waitlists move when offers get declined. You lose nothing by being on the list while you're in a hostel.
  4. Line up viewings before you land
    In-person applicants beat remote ones every time. Message listings for the week you arrive, not the week you're booking.
  5. Budget for it properly
    Four weeks of temporary housing, plus a deposit of one to two months' rent, plus your first month. That's the real arrival cost.

Walk away if you see this

  • Any request for money before a signed contract
  • A landlord who says they're abroad and will post the keys
  • Rent far below everything else in that city
  • No photos of the inside, or photos that appear on other listings
  • Pressure to decide today because "others are waiting"
  • A request to pay by bank transfer to a personal account before you've seen anything
