Germany · Arrival · First week

The order
is the whole thing

Every arrival task in Germany unlocks the next one. Do them out of sequence and you'll spend weeks waiting for something that takes one afternoon.

Why the order matters

Your address registration produces a certificate you need for nearly everything else. Your tax ID arrives automatically because of it. Your bank wants it. Your insurer wants it. Your university wants proof of insurance before it will enrol you.

Address → Anmeldung → Tax ID → Bank → Insurance → Enrolment → Residence permit

Before you fly

Two things you can do from home that save you weeks on the ground.

00 Book the Bürgeramt appointment from India

In Munich and Berlin, appointments book out two to four weeks ahead. If you already have a confirmed address, book the slot before you leave India. This single step is the difference between registering in week one and registering in week four.

Only possible with a fixed address If you're arriving into a hostel, you can't do this yet — you register once you're in a place that can issue you a landlord confirmation.
00 Sort insurance in advance from India

Some providers let you register before arrival and activate once you land. Registering early with a public insurer can give you a window of cover from your arrival date rather than from the day you finally get round to it. You'll finish activation once you have a bank account and enrolment certificate.

Week one, in order

German law expects you to register within 14 days of moving into an address. Treat that as the deadline everything else hangs off.

01 Anmeldung day 1–14

Address registration at the Bürgeramt or Einwohnermeldeamt. You walk out with a Meldebestätigung — the certificate you'll be asked for again and again. Nothing else on this page works properly without it.

Bring
  • Passport with visa
  • Rental contract or landlord confirmation (Wohnungsgeberbestätigung)
  • Completed Anmeldungsformular — download it from your city's Bürgeramt page beforehand
Do this first, not third Book the appointment the day you have an address. Slots don't appear on demand.
02 Tax ID arrives by post 2–4 weeks later

Your Steueridentifikationsnummer is assigned automatically when you complete the Anmeldung and posted to your registered address. You don't apply for it. If it hasn't arrived by week four, you can request it directly from the Finanzamt.

Check your letterbox name German post is delivered by surname on the box. If your name isn't on it, the letter goes back. This is the single most common reason people never receive their tax ID.
03 German bank account week 1–2

You need it to pay your insurance premium by direct debit, to receive wages if you work, and for daily life. Traditional banks usually want your address registration certificate and tax ID. Online banks are faster and more English-friendly — several offer German IBANs and can be opened largely online.

If you came through a package provider Blocked account packages often bundle a bank account. Check what you already have before opening a second one.
04 Activate health insurance week 1–2

Mandatory from your first day of residence in Germany. No exceptions. Send your insurer a copy of your registration certificate and your bank details so they can set up the direct debit. They then confirm your cover electronically to your university.

The circular bit nobody explains Insurance activation usually needs enrolment, and enrolment needs proof of insurance. Insurers resolve this by sending the university a digital confirmation of your registration with them — that's what counts as proof. Ask your insurer to send it if enrolment is blocking you.
05 Enrol at your university week 1–3

Immatrikulation. You'll need proof of statutory health insurance, your passport, admission letter, and the semester fee payment. You get your student ID and semester ticket out of it.

Usually required
  • Admission letter
  • Passport with visa
  • Proof of health insurance (sent digitally by your insurer)
  • Proof of semester fee payment
  • Address registration certificate
06 Residence permit before your visa expires

Your entry visa is temporary. You convert it into a residence permit at the Ausländerbehörde, and you'll need your registration certificate, enrolment certificate, insurance confirmation and proof of finances. Book this appointment early — waits are long in most cities and your visa expiry doesn't move.

07 Social security number 4–6 weeks

Registering with a statutory insurer automatically enters you into the German social security system, and your number arrives by post. Called both Sozialversicherungsnummer and Rentenversicherungsnummer — same thing, two names. You'll need it if you take a part-time job.

Realistic timeline

What actually happens versus what people expect. Most of the delay is post, not paperwork.

  • Day 1–2
    SIM card, and confirm your Anmeldung slot
    A German number makes every appointment booking easier.
  • Day 3–14
    Anmeldung done, certificate in hand
    Photograph it. You'll be asked for it repeatedly.
  • Week 1–2
    Bank account open, insurance activated
    These two unlock each other and enrolment.
  • Week 2–4
    Tax ID arrives by post
    Automatic. Just make sure your name is on the letterbox.
  • Week 4–6
    Social security number arrives
    Needed for any part-time work.

What actually goes wrong

  • Not booking the Bürgeramt slot early enough. Two to four week waits in big cities. Book it the day you have an address.
  • Name not on the letterbox. Your tax ID, insurance card and bank cards all arrive by post. No name, no delivery.
  • Trying to open a bank account before Anmeldung. Most traditional banks will turn you away without the certificate.
  • Leaving the residence permit appointment until the visa is nearly expired. The queue does not care about your deadline.
  • Assuming your travel insurance covers you as a student. It doesn't. Universities require statutory or recognised student cover.
  • Only carrying one copy. Photograph and back up every certificate the moment you receive it.

Verify everything with your own city and university

Procedures, waiting times and required documents vary by city, university and insurer, and they change.
