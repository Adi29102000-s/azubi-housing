Money you get back

You've already paid this. It's sitting in your kitchen.

  • Pfand — bottle depositYou get it back

    Almost every plastic bottle and can in Germany carries a deposit you paid at the till. Take them back to any supermarket, feed them into the machine, and you get a receipt to redeem at checkout. Throwing bottles in the bin is throwing away money you already spent.

    How to spot itLook for the Pfand symbol on the label, and check the shelf price — the deposit is listed separately. Glass bottles usually carry a smaller deposit than plastic.
  • The €1 trolley coinYou get it back

    Supermarket trolleys are chained. You insert a €1 coin to release one and get it back when you return the trolley. It isn't a charge — but if you don't have a coin, you're carrying your shopping by hand.

    The fixKeep a €1 coin in your bag permanently, or buy a plastic trolley token. Almost every German has one on their keys.

Money that quietly leaves

The costs nobody includes when they tell you Germany is affordable.

  • Rundfunkbeitrag — the radio fee€18.36 / month

    A mandatory broadcasting fee charged to every household in Germany, whether or not you own a TV, a radio, or any device at all. Nationality and visa status make no difference — international students pay it like everyone else. It's usually billed quarterly at €55.08.

    If you're in a WG, don't be the one who pays aloneThe fee is per household, not per person. Four flatmates pay it once between them and split it. In student halls it depends on the housing type — single self-contained apartments count as separate households. Sort this out with your flatmates the week you move in.
  • Shopping bagsEvery time

    Bags aren't free and nobody packs your shopping for you. You'll also be expected to bag it fast — German checkouts move quickly and the queue behind you notices.

    The fixKeep a folded cloth bag in your rucksack. It costs you once.
  • Sunday closuresCosts you indirectly

    Supermarkets close on Sundays. If you haven't shopped by Saturday evening you're eating out, and eating out is where a student budget actually breaks.

    The fixSaturday is shopping day. Treat it as fixed, not optional.
  • Cash-only placesCosts you in fees

    Plenty of small shops, bakeries and restaurants still take cash only. Getting caught out means using a foreign card at a random ATM and paying for it.

    The fixCarry €20–30 in cash, and only withdraw from your own bank's machines.

The expensive mistakes

These are the ones that cost hundreds, not cents.

  • Blocked account transfer feesCan cost you a visa

    Send exactly the required amount and the bank's transfer fee comes out of it — leaving you short. A shortfall in the blocked account is a rejection reason.

    The fixSend a buffer above the required figure, and confirm the exact current amount with the German Mission before transferring.
  • Sending money before signing anythingDeposit gone

    Housing scams target new arrivals every intake. Money requested before a viewing or a signed contract is the single clearest warning sign.

    The ruleNo signature, no money. A landlord claiming to be abroad who'll post you the keys is not a landlord.
  • Choosing private health insurance as a studentHard to reverse

    Private cover can look cheaper at first, but switching back to public later as a student is difficult and sometimes not possible. It's one of the few decisions here you can't easily undo.

    The fixUnderstand both options properly before choosing, and ask your university's International Office what most students there do.

One warning about letters

You'll get a real letter from the Beitragsservice asking you to register for the broadcasting fee. It looks alarming and it is genuine — ignoring it doesn't make it go away, it produces reminders and eventually enforcement.

But new arrivals are also targeted by fraudulent letters designed to look official. Before paying anything unexpected, check the sender against the official domain and ask a German-speaking friend or your International Office. Never pay from a letter alone if anything about it feels off.

Do these in week one

Ten minutes each, saves you all year

  • Put a €1 coin or trolley token in your bag
  • Put a folded cloth bag in your rucksack
  • Start collecting bottles instead of binning them
  • Agree with flatmates who pays the Rundfunkbeitrag and how you split it
  • Write your name on the letterbox — bank cards and official letters arrive by post
  • Find which ATMs belong to your own bank
  • Make Saturday your shopping day
