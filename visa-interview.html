They're testing whether you're a real student. Every question in the room is a version of the same one — do you actually know why you're going, and can you pay for it?

The odds are already in your favour

Around 90–95% of Indian student visa applications are approved. Rejections come from specific, avoidable mistakes — not competition. Don't walk in frightened; walk in prepared.

The questions, and how to answer them

Don't memorise these. Officers can tell instantly, and a rehearsed answer reads as coached. Understand the reasoning behind each one and answer in your own words.

Why Germany?
They're checking whether you researched, or just followed friends.
Weak"It's a good country." "Education is free." "Everyone is going there."
BetterName what's specific to your field — the industry presence, the research strength, the practical structure of German degrees. Tie it to what you studied before.
Why this university?
The one people fail. Everyone prepares "why Germany" and freezes here.
Weak"It's famous." "It has good rankings." "My agent suggested it."
BetterName the specific modules, a professor whose work matches your interest, the lab or research group, the industry links in that city. Concrete details prove you chose it.
Do this the night before Open your course page. Write down four modules, two professors, and one thing the department is known for. That's the whole preparation for this question.
Why this course?
They're checking academic consistency — does this follow from what you've already done?
Weak"It has good job prospects." A course unrelated to your degree with no explanation.
BetterConnect it to your previous degree and to where you want to end up. If it's a change of direction, say so and explain the reasoning — an honest pivot beats a vague answer.
How will you fund your studies?
Financial proof is the single biggest rejection reason. Know your own numbers.
WeakNot knowing the blocked account amount. Vague answers about family support.
BetterState the blocked account figure, who funds it, and what else supports you. Be able to say the number without checking your phone.
What will you do after graduating?
They want a plan that makes sense, not a promise.
Weak"I'll see what happens." Or anything implying you don't intend to leave regardless of outcome.
BetterDescribe a realistic path — the post-study work period, the kind of role you're aiming for, and what you'd bring back. Specific beats grand.
Why is there a gap in your education?
Only if you have one. Gaps aren't fatal — unexplained gaps are.
WeakHiding it, minimising it, or being visibly uncomfortable.
BetterSay what you did with the time and bring documents — work experience, courses, or a straightforward personal reason. Honesty handles this completely.
Do you speak German?
For an English-taught course this isn't disqualifying — but the answer says something about your seriousness.
Weak"I don't need it, my course is in English."
BetterState your actual level, and what you're doing about it. Learning German signals you've thought about living there, not just studying there.

Prepare this, not a script

The night before

  • Four modules from your course pageBy name. This alone answers half the questions.
  • Two professors and what they work onOne line each is enough.
  • Your blocked account number, from memoryNot on your phone. From memory.
  • Who is funding you and howParents, savings, loan, scholarship — be specific.
  • One sentence connecting your last degree to this oneThe consistency question, answered in advance.
  • Your city, and one thing about itIndustry, size, why it suits your field.
  • Read your own SOP againYour answers must match what you wrote. Inconsistency is what gets flagged.

What actually causes rejections

The interview is one factor. These are the others, and most are settled long before you walk in.

  1. Financial proof problems
    The biggest single cause. Wrong amount, currency conversion shortfalls, or funds arranged too late. Open your blocked account months ahead, not weeks.
  2. Missing APS certificate
    Mandatory for Indian applicants. It takes time. Start it early.
  3. Incomplete documents
    The mission can reject on this alone. Check the official checklist, not a blog.
  4. A weak or inconsistent SOP
    If your statement says one thing and your interview says another, that gap is what they notice.
  5. Course unrelated to your background, unexplained
    Changing field is fine. Not being able to explain why is not.
  6. A poor interview despite good documents
    Confused, inconsistent or unsure answers create doubt that correct paperwork can't undo.

Never say these

  • "There are no jobs in India." Or anything framing the move as escape rather than choice.
  • "I just want to leave this country." Same problem, said plainly.
  • Anything untrue. Fake documents or false statements can mean a long-term ban across the Schengen area, not just a rejection.
  • Arguing with the officer. The decision is theirs. Staying polite costs nothing; frustration confirms their doubts.
  • A memorised paragraph. They hear hundreds of these. Rehearsed answers stand out for the wrong reason.
  • Nothing. If you don't understand a question, ask them to repeat it. Silence reads worse than a pause.
