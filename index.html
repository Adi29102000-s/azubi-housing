🎒 Germany Day 1 to 5 Survival Checklist

Brought to you by your favorite content creator — Follow for daily survival tips!

🛬 Day 1: The Airport & Survival Mode

  • Get Emergency Cash: Withdraw at least €50–€100 in physical cash (small notes like €5, €10, €20) at the airport ATM. Many small German shops, bakeries, and ticket machines do not accept international credit cards.
  • Buy a Station Snack: Buy a big bottle of water and some snacks at the airport train station before you travel to your accommodation. If you arrive late or on a Sunday, local supermarkets will be completely locked.

🚉 Day 2: The Commute & Connectivity

  • Stamp Your Train Ticket: If you buy a physical paper ticket for local transit, look for the red or yellow box (Entwerter) on the platform. Stamp it before boarding to avoid a €60 fine.
  • Initiate SIM Activation: Buy a prepaid SIM card (ALDI TALK, LIDL Connect, or Vodafone) at a grocery checkout. Open the app and start the Video-Ident verification immediately using your passport.
    • Pro-Tip: If the online video call fails due to your visa type, take the printed registration form to the nearest Deutsche Post branch, and they will verify your identity in person for free.

🏢 Day 3: The Bureaucracy Baseline

  • Book Your Anmeldung Appointment: You legally must register your address within 14 days. Visit your local Bürgeramt website to secure an appointment for your City Registration (Anmeldung). Slots fill up weeks in advance!
  • Download Camera Translate: Download the Google Translate app and download the German language pack for offline use. Use the "Camera" feature to instantly read letters, recycling bin rules, and grocery labels.

🏦 Day 4: Unlocking Your Money

  • Open a German Bank Account: You cannot unlock your Blocked Account (Sperrkonto) without a local IBAN. Open a digital bank account (like Expatrio, Fintiba, N26, or Tomorrow) to fast-track the process.
  • Link the Blocked Account: Submit your new German IBAN to your blocked account provider so they can trigger your first monthly payout.

♻️ Day 5: Local Customs Mastery

  • Learn the Pfand System: Do not throw plastic bottles or soda cans in the regular trash. Look for the recycling loop logo. Take them back to any supermarket's automated machine to get €0.25 back per bottle as a voucher for your groceries.
  • Sort Your Garbage: Germany is incredibly strict about recycling. Separate your trash immediately into Blue (Paper), Yellow (Plastic/Packaging), Green/Brown (Organic food waste), and Black (Residual waste). Mixing them up can upset your landlord or neighbors!
