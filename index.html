Does your university give you a semester ticket?

Not every German university does — Bavaria doesn't, and several others voted it down. Where it exists, nationwide travel is already inside your semester fee, and it switches on when the semester starts, not when you land. Where it doesn't, you're buying the regular Deutschlandticket yourself at €63 a month. Find your university below.

The rule behind all of this

The Deutschlandsemesterticket comes from a federal–state framework agreement, not a law. That distinction is the whole reason this page needs to exist: nobody is obliged to offer it.

The price is fixed at 60%

Where the ticket exists, its price is 60% of the regular Deutschlandticket. That went from €58 to €63 in January 2026, so the student rate went from €34.80 to €37.80 a month — €226.80 per semester.

Each university decides separately

Your student body (AStA or StuRa) negotiates with the regional transport authority. Some hold a vote. Some vote no. Bavaria runs a different state-subsidised model entirely.

Private universities usually can't

The contract normally runs through a statutory student body, which most private universities don't have. Where a private university has signed its own contract, students get it. Most haven't.

You pay it either way

Where it exists it's compulsory — a line inside your semester contribution, not an optional purchase. Not using it doesn't get you out of paying for it.

When it actually starts

The ticket is tied to the semester period — 1 October to 31 March for winter, 1 April to 30 September for summer. If you land in September, plan on paying your own fares until your ticket switches on.

How first-semester students are handled varies by university

Some universities activate the ticket from the official semester start date regardless of when you enrol. Others start it from your enrolment date. RWTH Aachen states the 1 October / 1 April rule explicitly; there is no single national rule that covers everyone. Ask your own student office before you book September travel around it.

Check your own university in five minutes

  1. Search "[your university] Semesterbeitrag" — that's the semester contribution page, and it's where the fee breakdown lives.
  2. Look for a line item called Mobilitätsbeitrag, Deutschlandsemesterticket or Semesterticket. If €226.80 appears, you have it.
  3. No such line? Search "[your university] AStA Semesterticket" — the student body page will say whether a contract exists or a vote failed.
  4. Still nothing? Assume you don't have one and budget €63 a month, then confirm by email with the student office.
  5. Fees change every semester. Whatever you find, check the date on the page before you rely on it.

Getting the fee back

Where the ticket is compulsory, you can usually apply to your student body for a refund of the mobility fee. Grounds accepted at most universities:

  • Leave of absence for the semester
  • Semester abroad or a mandatory internship outside the ticket's area
  • Exmatriculation before or during the semester — full or pro-rata
  • Late enrolment, refunded for the months you weren't enrolled
  • Already holding a valid semester ticket from another university
  • Severe disability pass holders, or certified inability to travel
  • Social hardship, through the student body's hardship fund

Simply not using the ticket is not a ground anywhere. Deadlines are strict and usually tied to the re-registration window, so apply early.
