Your share: €6.12You each save €146.88 a year versus paying separately.

Let's be accurate about this This is not a loophole and not a trick. It is what the law says — §2 of the Rundfunkbeitragsstaatsvertrag charges one contribution per dwelling, whatever the number of residents. You are not dodging anything. You are avoiding a duplicate charge that the system creates because it writes to every newly registered resident automatically.

First: which one are you?

🏠 You share a flat, one front door A normal WG. Several bedrooms behind one entrance door, shared kitchen and bathroom. One person pays for everyone. The rest give that person's Beitragsnummer when the letter arrives. You don't pay separately
🚪 Dorm room with its own door off a corridor Most Studierendenwerk single rooms. If you reach your room from a shared corridor or stairwell rather than through a shared flat door, the law usually counts your room as its own dwelling. Everyone on the corridor pays their own €18.36. You pay in full
🔑 Your own studio or apartment One flat, one resident, one contribution. Nothing to split. You pay in full
🎓 You receive BAföG Full exemption on application, with your BAföG decision letter attached. Most international students are not eligible for BAföG, so this route is usually closed — check before you rely on it. Exempt on application

What to do when the letter arrives

Ask whoever already pays for the flat. It is a 9-digit number printed at the top of every letter from the Beitragsservice, and on their bank statement reference.

If nobody in the flat is registered yet, one of you has to register. The law gives you four weeks from moving in. Whoever registers becomes the payer and the others attach themselves to that number.

Go to rundfunkbeitrag.de and find Abmelden (deregister). Choose the reason that says another person already pays the contribution for this dwelling — „Die Wohnung ist bereits angemeldet".

The site is German-only. Use your phone's browser translate, or Google Translate's camera on the screen.

You need your own Beitragsnummer from the letter they sent you, and your flatmate's Beitragsnummer that already covers the flat. The address on both must match exactly, including the flat number.

Give the date you moved in as the start date, so any charge is cancelled from the beginning rather than from today.

If the online form rejects you — common when your address is written slightly differently on the two accounts — post or email the template below. Email: the contact form on their site. Post: ARD ZDF Deutschlandradio Beitragsservice, 50656 Köln.

Keep a copy and a screenshot of the confirmation. If a reminder arrives later, that screenshot is what ends the argument.

Processing takes a few weeks. Do not assume it worked until a letter says your account is closed. If a payment demand arrives in the meantime, reply referencing your deregistration rather than ignoring it.

Before you try anything clever

Never use a number that isn't real Quoting the Beitragsnummer of someone who does not actually pay for your flat is a false declaration, not a hack. The Beitragsservice cross-checks against the Einwohnermeldeamt — the same registration you did at your Anmeldung — so the addresses have to match. Getting caught means back-payment plus a penalty, and it is exactly the kind of thing that shows up when you apply to extend a residence permit.
Ignoring the letters does not work The contribution is a legal obligation, not a subscription. There is no cancelling it because you own no TV, and no opting out because you only watch Netflix. Unpaid amounts become an enforceable debt collected by the local authority, and they can be claimed back up to three years. Students who binned the letters in first semester get a four-figure demand later.
Low income alone is not enough There is a hardship exemption under §4(6), but it applies in narrow cases — mainly where a benefit was formally refused because your income exceeded the threshold by less than €18.36. Being a broke student is not by itself a qualifying reason, and blanket claims that internationals can get exempted are wrong. If you think you have a case, take it to your university's social counselling service.

Register, then deregister

The safe order is always: respond to the letter, name the paying flatmate, keep the confirmation. Silence is the only version of this that costs you money.
