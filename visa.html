The one that gates the rest

Get this before anything else

Ausbildungsvertrag

Your signed vocational training contract from a German employer. No contract, no visa appointment, no application. Everything on this page happens after you have it.

Watch out: a company-based dual training contract is not the same as a school-based acceptance letter. For healthcare roles especially, confirm which one you have — people get rejected on this alone.

Don't have a contract yet? There's a separate visa that lets you come and look for a training place. Under 35, up to nine months, same language requirement.

The full document list

What the embassy asks for alongside your contract. The mission can request more — this is the baseline, not a guarantee.

  • 01
    German language certificate

    B1 is the usual minimum. B2 is expected for nursing and healthcare, and makes you far more competitive everywhere else.

    The certificate is not enough on its own — you have to actually speak at the interview.
  • 02
    Valid passport

    With enough validity to cover your intended stay, plus passport photos to biometric spec.

  • 03
    Academic certificates

    Class 12 completion at minimum. Apostille and certified translation are usually required — allow several weeks for this.

  • 04
    Proof of finances

    Either your training salary covers living costs, or you top up the shortfall with a blocked account. See the numbers below.

  • 05
    Health insurance

    Cover must be active from your arrival date, not your training start date.

    A gap of even a few days between landing and cover starting is a common rejection reason.
  • 06
    Medical fitness certificate

    Confirming you can meet the physical demands of the profession you've chosen.

  • 07
    Police clearance certificate

    From India, clean, and recent enough to satisfy the mission.

  • 08
    CV and motivation letter

    Your reasoning has to hold together. A science student applying for a baking Ausbildung needs to explain why, convincingly.

  • 09
    Training plan (Ausbildungsplan)

    Shows the embassy what you'll actually learn and that the programme is structured and real.

The money question

The most misunderstood part of the whole process — because for Ausbildung, unlike a student visa, you often don't need the full blocked account.

Blocked account (Sperrkonto)

€11,904
for the year · about €992 per month

This is the standard 2026 figure. But if your Ausbildung salary already covers your living costs, it is often waived — you may only need to cover the shortfall, or nothing at all.

Do the arithmetic yourself. Compare your year-one monthly stipend against the threshold. Many applicants skip this check and get caught out. Confirm the exact figure with the mission handling your case, since the training-visa amount can differ.

Timeline

Start earlier than feels necessary. Document authentication and appointment waits are what kill applications, not the documents themselves.

  • 12 months out
    Start learning German
    A1 to B1 takes roughly 8–10 months of consistent study. A1 to B2, 12–14 months.
  • 6 months out
    Apply for training positions
    Employer job boards, the Federal Employment Agency, and IHK and HWK chamber listings.
  • On signing
    Start document authentication
    Apostille and certified translation take weeks. Begin the day your contract is signed.
  • Immediately after
    Book the embassy appointment
    Waits are long. Book before your documents are finished, not after.
  • Then wait
    Processing: several weeks to a few months
    Apply well before your training start date. Applying after it has begun is a common and fatal mistake.

Why applications actually get rejected

  1. German, not money. The most-cited reason. A B1 certificate is worthless if you can't hold the interview in German.
  2. Wrong contract type. School-based acceptance letter instead of a company-based dual training contract.
  3. The financial arithmetic doesn't add up. Stipend below the threshold with no blocked account covering the gap.
  4. A motivation letter that makes no sense. No credible link between your background and your chosen trade.
  5. Timeline errors. Applying after training has started, or insurance that begins after your arrival date.
  6. Incomplete documents. The mission can reject on this alone, and missing the interview counts too.

Verify everything against the official checklist

Figures and requirements change, and the German Mission can request additional documents at any time. Submitting everything on this list does not guarantee a visa.
