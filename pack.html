Half your suitcase
is wasted space

You get about 30 kilos total. Most people spend a third of it on things sitting in a shop ten minutes from their room — and leave behind the things that actually matter.

Leave it

All of this is available in Germany, often cheaper, and all of it is heavy or bulky. This is where your wasted third goes.

  • ✕Bedsheets, towels, pillows

    The single biggest space waster. You can buy all of it in your first week, and German duvet sizes don't match Indian bedding anyway — so half of what you bring won't fit the bed you end up with.

  • ✕Shampoo, soap, toothpaste, detergent

    Heavy, liquid, and sitting on every dm and Rossmann shelf. Bring a travel size for the first two days and nothing more.

  • ✕Kitchen steel, pressure cooker, plates

    Heaviest thing people pack. Most dorms come furnished with kitchen equipment, and second-hand furniture and utensils are close to free here. Check what your accommodation provides before you pack a single vessel.

  • ✕Rice, atta, dal

    Every German city with a university has an Indian or Asian shop. Bulk groceries are the worst possible use of 23 kilos.

  • ✕The winter jacket you bought in India

    It's the wrong jacket. Indian "winter wear" is built for a different cold. Buy a proper one here in the end-of-season sales, and bring layers instead — thermals, socks, a scarf.

  • ✕Umbrellas, hangers, storage boxes, stationery

    Cheap here, bulky in a bag, and you'll want to choose them once you've seen your room.

Bring it

Cheaper in India, hard to replace here, or genuinely painful to be without in month one.

  • ✓Your medicines

    The most important line on this page. Getting a doctor's appointment in Germany takes weeks, and many things sold over the counter in India need a prescription here. Bring your prescriptions in English along with the medication, and keep it in your cabin bag.

  • ✓Glasses and contact lenses

    Opticians here are expensive. Bring a spare pair and a copy of your prescription.

  • ✓Spices, masala mixes, ready pastes

    Available here but at a markup, and the blends you actually use often aren't. Sealed commercial packaging only — not loose powder in a pouch.

  • ✓Ethnic and formal clothes

    Far more expensive in Germany and you'll want them for Diwali, weddings and university events. One or two outfits, not the wardrobe.

  • ✓Small tools — good scissors, nail cutters

    Cheap in India, oddly expensive here. Checked bag only.

  • ✓Universal adapters and a power strip

    German sockets are Type C and F. One adapter plus an Indian power strip means you charge everything off a single adapter.

Food: the rule that catches people out

No meat. No dairy. No exceptions.

If you travel to the EU from a non-EU country, you cannot bring any meat or dairy products with you. Undeclared meat or dairy is confiscated and destroyed, and you can be fined or prosecuted.

  • Ghee is dairy. So is paneer, khoya, milk powder, and any homemade sweet made with milk.
  • Homemade food with meat or dairy falls under the same rule — home-cooked doesn't exempt it.
  • Dry prasad is generally fine. Dairy-based prasad is not.
  • Sealed vegetarian packaged food, snacks, tea and commercially packaged spices are generally fine.
  • Limited quantities of fruit, vegetables, eggs, egg products and honey are permitted.
  • Declare food if you're unsure. Declaring costs you a conversation; not declaring costs you a fine.
Loose spices are a security problem, not just a customs one Keep spices sealed in commercial packaging in your checked bag. Loose powder in an unmarked pouch invites a bag search at every checkpoint.

Cabin bag — documents only

If your checked bag is lost, this is what saves you

Bags get delayed more often than people expect, and none of these can be reissued from Germany in a hurry.

  • Passport with visa
  • Admission letter
  • Blocked account confirmation
  • Health insurance proof
  • Accommodation confirmation
  • Original academic certificates
  • Your medicines, with prescriptions in English
  • One change of clothes
Back everything up Photograph every document and put copies in your email before you fly. Costs five minutes, saves a month.

The 15 minutes that saves you most

Before you pack anything, email your accommodation and ask three questions.

  • 1Is the room furnished?

    Bed, mattress, desk, wardrobe — assume nothing. This changes what you buy in week one, not what you pack.

  • 2What's in the kitchen?

    Most dorms provide equipment. If yours does, that's several kilos you don't carry.

  • 3What size is the bed?

    German duvet and mattress sizes differ from Indian ones. Answer this and you'll know whether bedding is worth carrying at all — it usually isn't.

Check customs rules yourself before you fly

Customs and food import rules change, and restrictions can tighten during animal disease outbreaks. Confirm current rules with the European Commission and German customs before you pack food. Baggage allowances vary by airline. Nothing on this page is sponsored.
