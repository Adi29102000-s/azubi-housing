Field guide · Setting up in Germany

Where to buy it once you land

A pressure cooker and a mixer are about seven kilos of your baggage allowance, spent on things that are sitting on a shelf here. This is where those shelves are, what things roughly cost, and the one route that beats every shop in the country.

Household goods cost the same in Aachen as in Berlin. Only the Indian grocery shops change by city.

01

Two different problems

People search "prices in my city" for both. Only one of them varies.

Kitchen equipment — national

Action, TEDi, Woolworth, IKEA, Kaufland, Media Markt and Amazon charge the same everywhere in Germany. There is no cheaper city. Wherever you land, the same shops are within reach.

Indian groceries — local

This is what actually varies. Big cities have several Indian shops; small university towns may have none, and you order online instead. Delivery covers the whole country, so nobody is stuck.

On prices

The ranges on this page are indicative, gathered in August 2026 to help you decide what to leave behind — not quotes. Prices move, and discounters rotate stock constantly. Check before you buy.

02

Kitchen and appliances

Roughly cheapest to most expensive. The first two are where most students end up.

Action Discount chain Cheapest for basics

Dutch discounter, everywhere in Germany, and the single best place to kit out a kitchen from nothing. Pans, plates, cutlery, storage, cleaning supplies, bedding, extension leads — usually a few euros each. Stock rotates constantly, so what's there this week may be gone next week. Go in your first week and buy the boring things in one trip.

TEDi · Woolworth Discount chains Same bracket as Action

Same idea, similar prices, different stock. Worth walking into whichever is closest rather than hunting for a specific one. Between these three you can furnish a student kitchen for very little.

IKEA Nationwide Better quality, still cheap

Where you go for things you want to last — a decent pan, a proper knife, shelving, a desk lamp. Prices are published online so you can plan before you arrive. The catch is location: most stores are out of town, so budget a trip and a way to carry it home.

Kaufland · Rewe · Real Supermarkets Convenient, mid-price

Larger branches carry cookware and small appliances alongside food. More expensive than the discounters but you're already there. Kaufland's larger stores are the most likely supermarket to stock a pressure cooker.

Media Markt · Saturn · Amazon.de Electronics For appliances specifically

Where you buy a blender, kettle, rice cooker or induction plate. Media Markt stocks mixers, including Indian-style mixer grinders in many branches — worth walking in before you order anything, because you can see the motor rating and jar type in person. Amazon is often cheaper and delivers to your door, which matters when you have no car and no furniture yet.

03

The route that beats every shop

Almost no guide mentions this, and it's how most students here actually furnish a room.

  • 01KleinanzeigenGermany's classifieds site, formerly eBay Kleinanzeigen. Enormous, used by everyone, and full of kitchen equipment for a few euros — or free. Filter by "Verschenken" (giving away) and your postcode. Students leaving the country in September and February dump everything they own here.
  • 02University "free your stuff" groupsMost German university cities have a Facebook group where departing students give away furniture and kitchenware. Search your city plus "Verschenke" or "free your stuff". Peak season is exactly when you arrive.
  • 03SozialkaufhausCharity department stores, present in most cities, selling donated household goods very cheaply. Search your city plus "Sozialkaufhaus" or "Gebrauchtwarenhaus". A whole kitchen for the price of one new pan.
  • 04FlohmarktWeekend flea markets, in every city, usually Saturday or Sunday morning. Cheap, and the closest thing to a normal shopping experience in a country where everything shuts on Sunday.
Timing is on your side

You arrive in late September — the same weeks that last year's students are leaving. That is the best possible time to be looking at Kleinanzeigen and university giveaway groups. Look before you buy anything new.

04

Indian groceries

This is the part that genuinely depends on your city.

Big cities — Berlin, Munich, Frankfurt, Hamburg, Cologne, Düsseldorf, Stuttgart — have multiple Indian and South Asian shops, usually clustered near the main station or in the neighbourhoods with the largest South Asian communities. Smaller university towns often have one Asian shop carrying a partial range, or none at all.

How to find yours in five minutes

  • Search Google Maps for "Indischer Supermarkt" plus your city. This finds more than searching in English does.
  • Also try "Asia Markt" — many carry Indian staples even when they're not Indian-branded, and they're far more widespread.
  • Ask in your university's Indian student WhatsApp or Facebook group. You'll get a better answer in ten minutes than any list can give you, and it'll be current.
  • Look near the Hauptbahnhof. In most German cities that's where the international grocers cluster.
Be realistic about the range

A physical Indian shop in a small town will have rice, dal, common spices and some frozen items. It will not have every regional masala you're used to. That's the honest reason to bring your own — not that Indian food is unavailable here, but that the specific things you want may not be.

06

So what should you still pack?

Short list, because the whole point is that most of it is here.

✅ Your masalas

Not because Indian food is unavailable — because the specific blends you actually use often aren't. Light, cheap, and the thing people miss most.

✅ Documents

Originals and attested copies. Irreplaceable from here, and every Behörde will want one.

✅ Prescription medicines

With the prescription. Many Indian over-the-counter medicines need a doctor here, and you won't have one in week one.

✅ Ethnic clothing

Expensive and limited here. If you'll go to one Diwali or one wedding, bring the outfit.

❌ Pressure cooker

Around 3–4 kg, and €20–45 here. The baggage cost is worth more than the saving.

❌ Mixer

Similar weight, similar logic — with one exception, in the FAQ below.

❌ Bedding and towels

Bulky, heavy, and a few euros at Action in your first week.

❌ Bulk rice and flour

Heavy, and in every Asian shop and most supermarkets here.

07

Questions

Will my Indian appliances even work here?

Electrically, yes — India and Germany both run 230V at 50Hz, so an Indian appliance works with a plug adapter. The argument against bringing them is weight and replacement cost, not voltage. That's worth knowing, because it means bringing one isn't wrong, just usually not worth the kilos.

Isn't a German blender different from an Indian mixer grinder?

Yes, and this is the honest exception. A standard European blender handles smoothies and soups well but is not built for the wet grinding an Indian mixer does — chutney, idli or dosa batter, fresh masala pastes. If you cook that way regularly, a proper Indian mixer grinder is a real appliance you'd miss. You can buy one here through Amazon or Indian online stores at roughly €60–120, or bring yours and spend the baggage. For most students who cook everyday dal and sabzi, a cheap blender is fine.

What's the cheapest way to set up a kitchen?

Kleinanzeigen and university giveaway groups first, Action and TEDi for whatever's left. You arrive in the same weeks last year's students are leaving, which is the best time of year for it.

Can I get everything Indian online?

Close to it. Eight stores ship Germany-wide, with free delivery from €19 to €49 depending on the store. Split an order with flatmates and it works out cheaper than a small shop.

Do the shops open on Sunday?

No. Almost everything in Germany is closed on Sundays — supermarkets, discounters, IKEA, all of it. Plan your first shop for a weekday or Saturday, and don't land on a Saturday night expecting to buy anything.

Is IKEA worth the trip?

For a few things that need to last, yes. For plates, a pan and cleaning supplies, Action is closer and cheaper. Most people end up doing both — Action in week one, IKEA once they've moved into a permanent room.
