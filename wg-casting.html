A WG viewing in Germany is an interview, and the people asking the questions are the ones who'll share your kitchen. Nobody checks your salary. They check whether they want to see your face at 8am.

What you're up against

WGs in Berlin, Munich, Hamburg, Cologne and Frankfurt are flooded with applications. A popular Berlin listing can pull 200 messages in 24 hours. Getting invited to the casting already means your message worked — most people never get that far.

The thing nobody tells you

This is why people prepare for the wrong thing

It's called a casting for a reason

Most listings are posted by the current tenants, not the landlord. So the people interviewing you aren't assessing your finances — they're deciding whether they want to live with you.

Often several candidates are invited at the same time, back to back or all at once, and shown around together. It's usually casual — coffee or a beer in the kitchen — and that informality is misleading. They're evaluating you the whole time.

Think of it less as a job interview and more as a first date with three people who already know each other.

The practical consequence: your documents matter far less than your personality. Bring the paperwork anyway — but don't spend your preparation time on it.

What they actually ask

Every question is really the same question: will living with you be easy or annoying?

Tell us about yourself
Erzähl mal was über dich

Have a friendly sixty-second version ready — who you are, what brought you to Germany, a hobby or two. Not your CV. They want a person, not a profile.

What's your daily routine?
Wie sieht dein Tagesablauf aus?

They're working out bathroom traffic and noise. Early riser, late worker, weekend sleeper — just be accurate. Nobody is judged for their schedule, only for lying about it.

What do you cook?

This one surprises Indian applicants most. It's not hostility — shared kitchens carry shared smells and shared mess. Answer it directly: yes you cook, yes it's spiced, and here's how you handle ventilation and cleaning up. Owning it works far better than downplaying it.

How do you feel about guests and quiet hours?

They're checking compatibility, not policing you. If the flat is quiet and you love late parties, that mismatch is better found now than in month three.

Have you lived in a WG before?

No isn't a problem. Say so, and follow it with how you'd handle cleaning and shared costs. That answers the real question underneath.

Why this city, why this flat?

Generic answers hurt here the same way they hurt in a visa interview. Mention something specific about their listing — it proves you read it.

How to get picked

Do

  • Ask them questions back — cleaning rota, guests, how they split groceries
  • Show interest in the people, not just the room
  • Say something specific about their listing
  • Be the considerate version of yourself, not a different person
  • Message a thank-you afterwards
  • Write in German if you can, even translated

Avoid

  • Treating it like a formal interview
  • Only asking about rent and the room
  • Pretending to be social if you aren't, or quiet if you aren't
  • Talking mainly to the person showing you around
  • Being visibly disappointed by the flat
  • Sending the same copy-pasted message to every listing
The mismatch test cuts both ways If the flat looks like a library, don't lead with your love of late-night parties. But equally — don't perform. A WG you had to fake your way into is one you'll be uncomfortable in for a year.

Ask these before you agree

  • Can I register my address here?The most important question on this page. No Anmeldung means no bank account, no tax ID, no residence permit. Ask it explicitly.
  • Kalt or warm?Is the rent quoted cold or warm — and what exactly do the Nebenkosten cover?
  • How much is the deposit and when is it due?WG deposits are usually one to two months. The legal cap is three months' cold rent, and you have a statutory right to pay it in three monthly instalments.
  • What's the notice period?And is there a minimum stay?
  • Am I on the contract, or subletting from the main tenant?This changes your rights considerably.
  • How does the Putzplan work?Asking this signals you take shared cleaning seriously — which is exactly what they want to hear.

If you keep getting rejected

It's normal, and it's not personal. One student in Zurich applied to nearly a hundred flats, got ten castings, and was rejected at the final stage nine times. Rejection at the casting stage is the standard experience, not a signal about you.

What to change: personalise every message instead of copy-pasting. Widen your search to neighbourhoods further out — less competition, longer commute. Try Zwischenmiete sublets as a bridge. And apply in German, even translated; response rates rise sharply.

If a listing asks for money before you've viewed the place or signed anything, walk away. That's not a WG, it's a scam.

Every WG is different — this is the pattern, not a rule

Formats vary from a casual coffee to a formal group casting, and deposits, notice periods and contract types differ by flat and city. Confirm the specifics with the tenants or landlord before signing anything.
